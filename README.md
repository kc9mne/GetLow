# GetLow

Subwoofer enclosure design and simulation that runs in your browser. No install, no build step.

## Run it

Open `index.html` in Chrome or Edge, or serve the folder:

```bash
python -m http.server 8765
```

then go to http://localhost:8765. The 3D view loads Three.js from a CDN, so it needs internet the first time.

### Publishing it as an installable app

GetLow can be served from any web server over HTTPS and installed from there (Chrome or Edge: *Install* in the address bar; Safari on a Mac: *File → Add to Dock*). Installed, it runs in its own window and works offline.

```bash
python tools/build-site.py
```

builds `build/getlow/` and `build/getlow-site.zip`: the app, an offline copy script (`sw.js`, versioned from the files' contents), the manifest and icons, and an `.htaccess` that stops Apache caching the files that announce a new version. Add `--with-pe` to include the Parts Express catalogue (prices are taken out, since they go stale). Upload the folder's contents, or the zip and extract it, to where it's served (e.g. `/var/www/html/getlow/` for `https://example.com/getlow/`; with Webmin, *Tools → Upload and Download* or *File Manager* can extract a zip). After you upload a changed build, open copies offer *Update now*, and installed copies pick it up the next time they open. To try a build first, serve `build/getlow` locally and open it with `?sw` (e.g. `http://localhost:8766/?sw`): the offline copy is otherwise off on local servers, so it never hides your edits while you work. The icons come from `python tools/make-icons.py`.

Physics tests: open `tests/tests.html`. It compares the solver with closed-form results (sealed Fc/Qtc, vented and PR tuning, quarter-wave pipe, sensitivity, volume math).

## What it does

- **Layout**: the side column holds the settings for the project in the selected tab. The main area shows what you read, in views under the summary, one big thing at a time: *Response* (the charts), *3D view* (or, for a line array, *Line array*: the 3D view beside a summary of the design), *Side view*, *Polar*, *Coverage* and *Level vs distance* (for any design), *Room* (placement map, response at the seat, room modes), *Build sheet* (panel drawings, sheet layout, cut list, volume breakdown, panel resonance, standing waves, port runs) and *Search results* (the optimizer and drivers-for-this-box lists). Views with several parts show one part at a time, full size, picked from a row of sub-tabs. A view only appears when it applies, opening a settings tab brings up its view (Array → Line array, Build → Build sheet, Tools → Room), and a finished search opens its results. The view you're on is saved with the project.
- **Projects in tabs**: every project opens in its own tab along the top. **+** asks what you're building (subwoofer, full-range or bookshelf speaker, or line array), then helps you pick the driver. You can search your library and the catalogue with a size filter and pick one, then choose an alignment and how many drivers. Or you can start from a box: give a net volume, or outside dimensions and a wall thickness (e.g. 20 × 4 × 4 in with ¼" walls), choose the alignment, and rank by depth, output or a balance. The alignments are sealed, ported (the best tuning and a port sized for air speed, for each driver), passive radiator (one or two from the catalogue, or whichever works better: one with 1.2–3× the driver's cone area, or two with 0.6–1.6× each, the smallest that together move twice its air and fit beside the driver, tuned with added mass), and for subwoofers 4th-order and 6th-order dual-reflex bandpass (the best split between the chambers and the best tunings). Set how many drivers share the box (8 to start for line arrays, where the box is the whole column); they're wired in parallel, and the volume, ports and passive radiators are sized for all of them together. Every driver of exactly that nominal size is simulated in the box. Drivers that don't fit the front (or the divider) or are too deep are left out. When you give outside dimensions, the best results are also laid out in that exact box, with ports routed, drivers on their faces and room for the motor, and any that don't really fit are skipped. The top three are shown with their response curves, and *Use this one* opens the project with that driver in exactly that box. For example, a full-range speaker starts standing free with the charts to 20 kHz, and a line array opens on the Array tab. The selected tab is always on the graphs; tick up to three more to overlay them, each keeping its own colour on every chart. Double-click the selected tab's name to rename it, and close a tab with × (with an undo). *Open…* loads one or more project files, each into a tab of its own, and *Save project* saves the selected tab. Open tabs (and the curves of the ones you're not on) are kept between sessions. With none open, GetLow shows a start page.
- **Drivers**: imports WinISD `.wdr` files, Hornresp records and GetLow `.json`, by button or by dropping files on the window. You can edit parameters, create new drivers and export `.wdr`. The library is kept in browser storage.
- **Driver catalogue**: *Browse catalogue…* on the Driver tab searches about 1,800 current drivers and 84 passive radiators (Dayton, GRS, Tang Band, Eminence, SB Acoustics, Peerless, B&C, Faital, JL Audio, Scan-Speak, SEAS and more). You can filter by type and size, sort by Fs, Xmax or Vd, and add one with a click. Dual voice coil drivers come with how their specs were published. Passive radiators fill a PR card from *Pick from catalogue…*. The data comes from the [OpenISD](https://github.com/Johnlon/openisd) catalogue (MIT licence, see `data/OPENISD-LICENSE.txt`). To refresh it, pull OpenISD and run `python tools/build-catalogue.py PATH/TO/openisd`. Parts Express drivers that OpenISD lacks can be added from a scrape of parts-express.com (`git clone https://github.com/meowelectronics/parts-express-database`, then `python tools/build-pe-extra.py PATH`). That writes `data/catalogue-pe.js`, which is included in this repository. It holds the spec tables from the public product pages, with a link back to each one. The scrape it comes from has no licence of its own, and the published site build leaves the prices out (`tools/build-site.py --with-pe`). GetLow reads those pages with the same spec reader as *Paste specs*. It fixes a Vas that's off by a unit slip or rounding when Sd, Cms, Mms and Fs agree, fills a missing Sd from Vas and Cms (or estimates it from the frame size, tagged), and leaves out anything that still doesn't add up. In the list they're tagged PE (with their price where the copy has one), and Parts Express's own sealed and vented box suggestions go into the driver's notes.
- **Paste specs**: copy a spec table from Parts Express or a maker's datasheet and paste it (the *Paste specs…* button, or Ctrl+V anywhere outside a text box). It reads metric and imperial units (L / ft³ / in³, cm² / in² / m², mm / in, µm/N / mm/N, comma decimals, "1,600 W") and guesses missing units from plausible ranges. It also cross-checks Vas against Sd and Cms, and Fs against Mms and Cms, to catch mislabelled units. A review screen shows each value in both systems before you add it.
- **Send to GetLow bookmark**: drag the button from the Driver tab to your bookmarks bar. Clicked on a product page, it reads the page's spec tables, definition lists or label/value blocks, or just your selection if you've made one. When a page has no clear table, it sends the page text and GetLow picks out the T/S values. That makes it work for single lookups on sites like loudspeakerdatabase.com, which don't allow automated access: the bookmark only reads the page you opened yourself. When GetLow runs from a local server, the bookmark opens GetLow with the import ready; when GetLow is opened as a file, it copies the specs for you to paste. The bookmark stores GetLow's address, so drag a new copy if you move the folder.
- **Dual voice coil subs**: detected on import, including how the published specs were measured (coils in series, in parallel, or one coil). Each design picks series or parallel coil wiring.
- **Optimize tab**: pick a goal (the smallest box that reaches a target F3, the deepest F3 within a box size, or a weighted blend) and GetLow runs through sealed, ported, 4th-order bandpass, both 6th-order bandpass types and manifold designs (manifold needs an even driver count). That's about 2,500 simulations in under a second. Each design must stay within your allowed peaking, keep its port under the air-speed limit at full power (ports are sized up to suit), reach the top of your band, and have a port that really routes in a box of that size, as a full-span slot or a round tube, at a buildable size. If a slot only fits by running through the neighbouring chamber, the optimizer can use that route (there's a switch), and finalists whose drivers and ports don't fit side by side, with ¼" to spare, are skipped. The best few of each type are listed with F3, level and max SPL, and *Use* loads one (with a way back).
- **Drivers for this box** (Optimize tab): keeps the current box as built (volumes, ports, driver count and wiring) and simulates every driver in the catalogue and your library in it, allowing for each one's motor displacement. Drivers too big for the baffle, too deep, or that would stop a port fitting are left out. Filter by impedance (each coil), single or dual voice coil, and the load your amplifier will see with your driver count and wiring; dual-coil drivers are wired series or parallel to hit that load. It ranks them by deepest F3, most output over a band you choose (at your amplifier's power, capped at each driver's rating and limited by Xmax), or a balance of both, within your allowed peaking. The best ones go on the graph with their own colour and line pattern, on every chart. *Use in this box* swaps a driver in; *Save to compare* keeps it on the Compare tab.
- **Compare tab**: save any number of designs (driver, box and drive), each with its own colour and line pattern, and tick them to show them on the graph next to the current design, on every chart. Load one to keep working on it (with a way back), update it from the current design, rename or delete it. Saved designs go into the project file. *Load projects…* adds the designs from one or more saved project files (each file's own design, simulated with its own driver, box and drive, plus any designs saved in it), so past work drops straight in. *Save to compare* above the chart does the same as saving here, in one click.
- **Small speakers and full-range drivers**:
  - *Baffle and listening* (Enclosure tab): choose "in a wall" (half space, right for subs) or "standing free". Standing free adds the baffle step (up to 6 dB less bass below about 115 / baffle width Hz) and the ripple from the box edges, for the actual box size and driver position. An edge round-over smooths the ripple, and the driver can be moved off centre. F3 is still measured on the box alone.
  - Off-axis curves at 15 to 90° on the SPL chart, with each cone and port treated as a rigid piston (beaming, no cone break-up).
  - Baffle-step compensation: a coil with a resistor across it, in series with the driver, with values suggested from the baffle size and how many dB you want back. It's part of the simulation, so impedance and SPL include it.
  - L2R2 voice-coil model: enter L2 and R2 from the datasheet (or paste them) for a more accurate treble and impedance.
  - Choosing a driver smaller than about 5" sets the chart to run to 20 kHz.
- **Build sheet** (Build sheet view): every panel drawn to one scale with its size, which edge is which, and its cutouts dashed and numbered: driver and passive-radiator holes, round port holes (tube outside size), slot openings, the notch for a slot through a divider, and window-brace openings. A table under each drawing gives each hole's size and its position from the left and bottom edges. Each divider gets its own drawing. The driver cutout comes from *Cutout diameter* on the Driver tab (read from pasted specs too), or is estimated at 88% of the frame and marked so. *Sheet layout* nests the parts onto stock sheets (4 × 8 ft by default; set the size, the saw kerf, and whether parts can turn) and shows how many sheets you need. A port that doesn't fit is flagged, since its panels aren't real yet. *Print build sheet* opens a print-ready page (the design, cut list, panel drawings, port runs, sheet layout and an assembly checklist) to print, save as PDF, or save as a page.
- **Box materials and panel resonance** (Build tab): plywood, MDF, Baltic birch, aluminium, steel, acrylic, and 3D prints in PLA, PETG, ABS/ASA and resin, each with its stiffness, density and damping. For prints, set the solid skin (perimeters) and the infill. *Panel resonance* lists the first bending mode of every wall's free span (between corners and dividers) and marks the ones inside the band the speaker plays. Metal panels, which hardly damp themselves, get a warning and advice on damping layers. Prints get a reminder to seal them against leaks.
- **Room for the drivers and the chamber layout**: the box keeps every face big enough for what's on it (drivers, round ports and passive radiators in a row, plus the slots against its walls), using the placement's own numbers, and resizes the other dimensions to hold the volumes. That works whether you type a dimension, change a volume or let it settle. If a locked dimension stops it, a warning says so. The driver's motor must fit too: its depth plus about ¾" behind it for the pole vent. On the front baffle that sets the minimum depth; on a divider it sets the minimum length of the chamber the motor points into, which then gets longer (the box narrows to suit) while the other chambers keep their volumes. When the drivers need more room than the volumes allow, the chamber holds the extra air and a warning says how much, so you can glue in a filler block of that size to keep the tuning. Enter the driver's mounting depth for an exact check; otherwise it is estimated from the driver's size. Multi-chamber boxes get a *Layout* card on the Enclosure tab: chambers side by side (divider upright, drivers on it lined up along its height) or stacked (divider flat, drivers across the width, ports on the fronts of the top and bottom chambers). The card shows how big each layout comes out and whether everything fits in it.
- **Stuffing and standing waves**: each chamber card (Enclosure tab) has stuffing: polyester fibre fill, long-fibre wool, or a foam or felt lining (with how much of the volume or walls it covers, and the lining thickness). A fill makes the chamber act up to 15% bigger (polyester) or 20% (wool) and adds absorption, lowering the box Q. Both feed into the simulation. *Standing waves inside* (Build tab) lists the half-wave modes along each chamber's width, height and depth, marks those inside the band the speaker plays, says whether the driver's position drives each one or sits in its null, and whether the stuffing damps it. Undamped modes in the band get a warning. A fill of 30% or more damps them; a lining only works above about an eighth of a wavelength of its thickness. Woofers 7" and up with no low-pass set are taken as subs played to 200 Hz for this check and for panel resonance.
- **Line arrays** (Array tab): by default the array is one box, a column, with every driver in a line on its baffle. The element count is the design's driver count, the drivers sit at the spacing you set (starting edge to edge: outer diameter plus 2 mm; *Edge to edge* puts them back), and the column is just wide enough for them, as tall as the row and as deep as the volume needs. *Column width* and *Column height* hold the baffle at a size you choose (a taller or wider baffle makes the box shallower, with room below the drivers for ports); *Narrowest* and *Height follows the row* go back to automatic. Opening the spacing shows how the line breaks up at high frequencies: the continuous-line and grating-lobe limits, coverage and side view all follow it. Changing the driver count keeps each driver's share of the air. The other choice, *Separate cabinets stacked*, treats each element as the box on the Enclosure tab. Either way the line can be straight, arc, J, progressive (splays growing 1Δ, 2Δ, 3Δ … to a terminal angle) or your own splay angles. Set the element count and pitch, tilt, electronic tilt (delays), shading (Hann or Keele's CBT Legendre taper), The Array tab holds only the line's own design (*This design is a line array* turns it on for any project).
  - *Line array* view: the 3D box beside a summary of the design: the drivers (count, model, size, Fs/Qts/Vas, Xmax and total moving air, power, sensitivity, wiring and amplifier load, cone breakup), the line (length, spacing and gap or overlap, fill, shape, shading), how it behaves (continuous-line and grating-lobe limits, near-field reach, vertical coverage at 1, 4 and 10 kHz, level at the listener), the box (alignment, volume in all and per driver, Fc/Qtc or tuning, F3, outside size) and the tweeter line.
- **Listening position** (Signal tab): placement (free standing, on the floor with its reflection, or floor to ceiling with both), the height of the top of the speaker, ceiling, listener distance and ear height, smoothing, and *Show the response at the listening position on the SPL chart*. The side view, polar, coverage and level-vs-distance views work for any design: a line array uses its elements, other designs use their own front drivers where they sit on the baffle (or one source at the middle of the box for bandpass and horns).
  - *Side view*: by default four frequencies layered (500 Hz red, 1 kHz purple, 5 kHz green, 8 kHz blue, lowest underneath), each shaded where it is within 3, 6 or 10 dB of its loudest point at the same distance and outlined, so you can see the beam narrow and the gaps break it up as the frequency rises. Each layer can be switched off. *Single frequency* shows one frequency in bands 6 dB apart, measured down from the loudest spot. The array and listener are drawn in.
  - *Vertical polar* at several frequencies, *coverage angle* (−6 dB) against frequency next to a single element, and *level against distance* showing the near field (−3 dB per doubling) giving way to the far field (−6 dB).
  - Spacing limits: the array acts as one continuous line while the pitch is under half a wavelength; grating lobes appear once it passes a wavelength. The near-field reach is estimated at 1, 4 and 10 kHz.
  - *At the listening position* on the SPL chart sums every element, with that design's own response, at your listening spot, with optional 1/12 to 1/3-octave smoothing.
  - *Tweeter line*: a second line of domes or ribbons/AMTs along the same curve, centred on the main line, with a crossover (12 or 24 dB/oct, Butterworth or Linkwitz-Riley), tweeter level, polarity and delay. Each tweeter is modelled from its sensitivity, Fs and Qts. The SPL chart shows the total plus each line dashed, *Match the tweeter level* sets the level at the listener, and the panel warns when the main line's spacing or cone breakup sits below the crossover.
- **Cone breakup** (Driver tab): pick the cone material for an estimate of the first breakup peak (about K ÷ cone diameter), or enter the frequency, height and sharpness from the datasheet's response graph. The response gets the peak and the fall-off above it, and above breakup only the middle of the cone radiates, so it beams less than a rigid piston. It shows in the SPL chart, the off-axis curves and line arrays, and there's a warning when the peak falls inside the band the driver plays. F3 is unaffected. Saved with the driver.
- **Save project** writes everything: the design, box, signal, optimizer and matcher settings, room, Tools inputs, saved comparisons, units, and every driver used, so the file opens the same anywhere. Opening a project replaces all of that, with nothing left over from the one before.
- **Isobaric**: two drivers per position working as one (half the Vas, same Fs and Qts, about 3 dB less sensitive), clamshell or tunnel, with the pair wired in series or parallel. A tunnel's sleeve counts as displacement, and both are drawn in 3D.
- **Enclosures**: sealed, ported, passive radiator, 4th-order bandpass, manifold (drivers on two facing dividers, each with its own sealed rear chamber, firing into a shared vented middle chamber; acoustically a 4th-order bandpass), 6th-order bandpass (parallel/dual-reflex and series-tuned), 8th-order bandpass, transmission line / quarter-wave, and pipe / tube (front loaded, sealed or open back).
- **Results**: SPL, max SPL (limited by Xmax, PR Xmax and thermal power), impedance, cone and PR excursion, port air speed and group delay. There's an optional subsonic filter and car cabin gain, and you can overlay any number of saved designs (Compare tab).
- **Port noise**: air speed is checked at full power (rated Pe, or Xmax if that comes first, after any subsonic filter), not just at the chart's input level. Limits are about 17 m/s for quiet and 25 m/s for audible chuffing and compression, raised by up to 8 m/s when both ends of a round port are rounded over. When a port is too small, GetLow gives the smallest quiet size at the same tuning and its length.
- **True volume**: material thickness, double baffles, dividers, and the volume taken up by drivers, ports (including tube wall), slot-port walls, PRs and bracing. Net (acoustic) volume is kept separate from gross (internal) volume.
- **Dimension scaling**: with *Hold chamber volumes* on, changing one outside dimension resizes the unlocked ones so every chamber keeps its net volume. With it off, you type the outside size and see what you get inside. Golden-ratio and cube presets are included.
- **Horns**: back-loaded, front-loaded and tapped horns, built from one or more segments (exponential, hyperbolic-exponential with T, conical, parabolic). Each segment is solved exactly, so results hold to 20 kHz for small full-range drivers. Includes a rear chamber, a throat chamber, the mouth's radiation space (free air, floor/wall, wall plus floor, or corner) and a "design from cutoff" helper. The Build tab gives the area and height/diameter along the path, so you can mark out a folded horn.
- **Ports**: round ports with optional flares (a round-over radius at each end). Tuning, length and volume are calculated from the real flare shape, and air speed is checked at the narrowest point. Slot ports can have round-overs at both ends (the baffle opening and the panel end inside), and the tuning and noise limit account for them. Slot ports can also use the cabinet wall as one side and can run the full inside height, following the box when you resize it.
- **Port fit**: every port is laid out on its mounting face (the front baffle or a bandpass divider) and checked against the real chamber. A port too long to run straight gets up to two 90° bends (round) or up to two folds (slot): one fold along the far wall, then either a U back beside its first run or a wrap forward along the opposite wall. This works in any chamber. Slot folds can have a round-over on the inside corners and fillet blocks in the outer corners, so the width stays constant through the turn. Air then follows an arc round each fold, so the straight runs are lengthened to keep the tuning. Fillets go in the cut list, and both show in 3D. The open end always keeps at least one port width of clear space, and bends stay clear of the driver's motor and other ports. The 3D view draws the route (red if it can't fit), the Build tab lists each leg and adds the slot panels to the cut list, and buttons make the box deep enough to run a front port straight, shrink the port until it fits at the same tuning, or raise the tuning until it fits at the same size. Long ports also get their own pipe resonance (half-wave) listed, since the lumped simulation doesn't show it. Enter the driver's mounting depth for an exact check; otherwise it's estimated from the diameter.
- **Through-chamber slots**: in a multi-chamber box, a slot can open on the baffle of the neighbouring chamber. It runs back along that chamber's outer wall, along the back wall, through a notch in the divider, and opens inside its own chamber. This keeps the duct clear of a divider-mounted driver. The driver is moved forward on the divider to make room, the duct's volume is shared between the two chambers, and the Build tab gives the notch size.
- **Slots against any wall**: choose the side, top, bottom (or the back, for divider slots), or leave it to GetLow. Left to itself, a slot never goes against a divider with a driver, PR or port on it, and if it doesn't fit on its usual wall it tries the others and says which one it moved to. Every run of a slot is checked against the driver's motor and a clear zone in front of a divider-mounted cone. With full span, the slot panel runs between the two opposite panels, so it's a plain rectangle glued on three edges. That's the easiest slot to cut, it braces the box, and the wall saves a panel. New slots start this way.
- **3D view**: X-ray and exploded views, with chambers, dividers, drivers, ports and PRs.
- **Bracing**: window braces (frame width set by you) or dowels (side to side or top to bottom) per chamber. They count as displacement, show in 3D, go in the cut list (window braces with their opening size), and sit behind the driver's motor when the box is deep enough. Dowels move to a clear height when a port is in the way. A port through a window brace is flagged so you can notch it.
- **Measure your own T/S** (Tools tab): enter Re from a meter and the free-air impedance peak (Fs, Zmax, and the two frequencies where the impedance is √(Re·Zmax)). Then add either a known mass stuck on the cone and the new Fs, or the resonance in a sealed test box of known volume. GetLow works out Qms, Qes, Qts, Mms, Cms, Vas and BL, compares them with the datasheet, and saves them as a new "(measured)" driver. Sd comes from the datasheet or your measured cone diameter. It includes a helper for measuring impedance with a resistor and a meter.
- **Room modes and placement** (Tools tab): enter the room's length, width and height, how damped it is, where the sub and the seat are. GetLow lists the room's modes (marking where the sub or seat sits in a null, and modes close enough to stack into one boom), shows your design's response at the seat next to its outdoor response, and draws a top-view map of how smooth the bass would be with the sub (or the seat) at each spot on the floor. Click the map to move it there. One sub, or two mirrored across the width, front to back or in opposite corners. Optionally adds the in-room curve to the main SPL chart. It models an empty, closed, rectangular room below about 200 Hz, so confirm with a measurement.
- **EBP hint** on the Driver tab: under about 50 leans sealed, over about 100 leans ported, with a note when Qts is unusually high or low.
- **Cut list** in 1/16" fractions (or cm), plus a **pipe resonance calculator** under *Tools*.

## How the simulation works

Each design becomes a lumped-element acoustic network: chamber compliances with leakage (Ql) and absorption (Qa) losses, port masses with end correction and loss (Qp), passive radiators, and the driver as an electro-mechanical source. The network is solved at each frequency by complex nodal analysis. Lines and pipes are chains of distributed tube segments (48 steps, so tapers work), with a radiation impedance at the mouth. SPL is at 1 m, half space, using WinISD's air constants (ρ = 1.20095 kg/m³, c = 343.68 m/s).

Limits to know about:
- Horns are simulated unfolded (path length and areas only); bends start to matter in the upper midrange. A horn mouth in free air or a corner uses an image-source estimate. A mouth in a baffle (2π) uses the exact piston formula. The voice-coil inductance is a simple inductor, so the top octave or two of a full-range driver is approximate.
- Line stuffing uses a simplified damping model. Use it as a guide, not as a Martin King-grade result.
- Bent ports are simulated at their centre-line length. A bend's own small effect on tuning isn't modelled. Port compression is approximated only by the optional air-speed limit on max SPL.
- Slot end correction near cabinet walls uses an image-method estimate. Expect a real slot to land within a few Hz of the prediction.
- Cabin gain is a simple +12 dB/oct shelf, not a measured car transfer function.
- Baffle diffraction uses the edge-source model: each point on the baffle edge (spread evenly by angle around the radiator) re-radiates half the arriving pressure, inverted, with its extra path length. That gives the 6 dB step and the edge ripple. A round-over averages each edge's phase over its arc. Off axis, each radiator is a rigid piston, 2·J1(ka·sinθ)/(ka·sinθ).
- Panel resonance is the first mode of a rectangular plate, f = (π/2)(1/a² + 1/b²)·√(D/m) with D = E·t³/12(1−ν²) for simply supported edges, and about 1.8× that for clamped edges. A 3D-printed wall is solid skins over a partly filled core. Material values are typical ones and real batches vary.
- Line arrays follow Ureda, "Analysis of Loudspeaker Line Arrays" (JAES 52(5), 2004), with discrete elements instead of continuous lines: p = Σ wᵢ·D(θᵢ)·e^(−jk·rᵢ − jωτᵢ)/rᵢ, with each element a rigid piston in front and wrapping round its box at low frequency. A continuous straight line reproduces sin(z)/z and the quarter-power angle 2·asin(0.6λ/L), and an arc's coverage approaches its included angle; both are in the tests. The near-field reach uses (3/2)·f·L²·√(1 − 1/(3fL)²) (f in kHz, L in m). Floor and ceiling are perfectly rigid mirrors.
- Room response is the modal sum for a rigid rectangular room, p ∝ Σ ψn(sub)·ψn(seat) / (Λn·(ωn² − ω² + 2jδω)), with every mode decaying at δ = 6.9/RT60 and modes counted up to 2.5× the top frequency. It is given relative to the same sub at 1 m in half space, so it adds to the SPL curve in dB. The placement map scores each spot by the standard deviation of that curve over the band you choose.
- Starting volumes are worked out for one driver from its own Fs, Qts and Vas, then multiplied by the number of drivers: sealed at Qtc 0.707 (or 1.3 × Qts, up to 1.0, for drivers whose Qts is too high for that), ported with the QB3 volume and tuning. Bandpass "suggested start" values are built from those. They are only a starting point. Tune chambers and ports from there.
